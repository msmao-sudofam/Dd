# CGC / Generated film concepts

Two cinematic directions, three new standalone concept images, and twelve ready-to-adapt shot prompts. All images were made with the built-in image generator. These are **illustrative concepts**, not photographs or footage of completed CGC work. No video files have been generated in this package.

## Choose the film

| Direction | Idea | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Line** | A pencil stroke becomes the edge of a mullion, then the line of a sealant bead. One visual motif carries us from plans to finished glazing. | Contractor credibility; homepage opener; bid campaign |
| **The Reflection Run** | We begin inside a sliver of reflected light, travel along a facade, cross from reflection into real interior space, and reveal the finished building. | The most cinematic homepage intro; brand film; social launch |

**Recommendation: The Reflection Run for the opening film.** Use The Line as the companion film explaining the work. The camera is the creative effect: moving perspective, reflections and match cuts, rather than a building assembling like a game asset.

## Production rules for both films

Generate each shot as its own video clip; do not animate the entire six-panel storyboard sheet. Use the storyboard for direction and create a clean, unnumbered first-frame still for each shot if the video tool requires one. Never pass panel numbers, titles or gutters as part of the scene.

Keep one building reference per film. Lock the facade, number and spacing of bays, mullion finish, glass tint, door handles and sun direction. Keep screen travel left-to-right until the final pullback. Generate about five seconds per shot, then select the best four seconds in the edit; avoid forcing every transformation into one long generation.

Target master: **24 seconds, 16:9, 24fps**. Compose important action in the central 60% for a separately reframed 9:16 version. Generate at the highest native resolution available; do not describe a 720px source as 4K. Keep the website version muted, poster-first, with a pause control and a still reduced-motion presentation. Make a separately mixed social version with original or licensed sound.

No generated logos, readable plan text or company signage inside the clips. Add the real SVG logo and exact typography in post. No invented project locations, clients, certifications or performance ratings. Glass must remain rigid, properly supported and plausible. No unsupported floating lites, melting frames, exploding boards, sparks or phantom extra doors.

If published, identify the film as a **concept film** and keep it separate from the documented project gallery.

# Film A / The Line

**24 seconds. Six shots.** The visual thread is a single dark line. Palette: white paper and stucco, graphite aluminum, steel-blue glass. Morning light. Tactile, precise and confident. No voice-over required.

| Shot | Edit time | Camera and action | Transition | Sound idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A01 | 00–04s | Extreme macro tracks a pencil point laying one clean diagonal line on white plan paper. | Cut at the same angle to a real aluminum edge. | Dry pencil scratch; low tonal pulse begins. |
| A02 | 04–08s | Macro dolly along the sharp edge of a graphite mullion beside rough white stucco. | Edge wipes across the lens into the wide opening. | Soft metal contact; a short resonant click. |
| A03 | 08–12s | Slow lateral move reveals an unglazed storefront frame. Empty bays show the clean unfinished interior. | A mullion crosses frame and hides the cut. | Open room tone; two measured tool taps. |
| A04 | 12–16s | Two gloved hands guide a suction-supported glass lite into alignment. Camera stays outside its travel path. | Match the pane edge to the sealant joint. | Suction-cup latch; restrained glass-handling detail. |
| A05 | 16–20s | Extreme macro follows a nozzle laying a precise perimeter bead at the glass/aluminum joint. | The finished bead becomes a dark vertical in the final facade. | Soft continuous material contact; music opens up. |
| A06 | 20–24s | Slow pullback reveals the same completed facade. Interior lights and daylight read through the glazing. Hold the final frame. | Real logo and headline fade on in post. | Quiet resolution; one clean final note. |

## Master prompt prefix

Append this to each shot prompt: “Photorealistic live-action commercial cinematography, architectural glazing installation, same modest white-stucco commercial storefront as the supplied anchor reference, graphite aluminum frames and neutral steel-blue glass, believable scale and hardware, precise corrected verticals, coherent morning sunlight, tactile real materials, restrained film grain, 24fps, one controlled camera move, no CGI appearance, no text, no logos, no watermark, no extra people or tools. Preserve the reference geometry and do not invent new bays.”

## A01 / Drawing becomes material

> Extreme macro on a graphite pencil tip touching white architectural plan paper. Camera tracks smoothly parallel to the tip as it draws one straight line from lower left toward upper right. Fine paper fibers and graphite texture are visible; the background plan marks remain out of focus and unreadable. No rubbery pencil or bending paper. End with the dark line crossing the frame at a 35-degree diagonal so the next shot can match that angle. Five-second source clip, no cuts within the shot.

## A02 / The edge

> Start with the exact 35-degree diagonal from the prior shot, now the crisp edge of a graphite aluminum storefront mullion in extreme close-up. A slow macro dolly moves along its brushed finish beside textured white stucco. Rack focus gently from the metal edge to the mounting surface. The line is a real object, not a morph or animated extrusion. End as the mullion fills the right side of the lens for an editorial wipe. Five-second source clip.

## A03 / The opening

> Wide three-quarter eye-level view of the reference storefront with graphite aluminum framing installed but glass and door leaves absent. Slow left-to-right lateral dolly, revealing the depth of the empty openings and the unfinished clean commercial interior. Maintain identical facade and bay spacing to the final-frame anchor. No frame growth, no magically appearing hardware, no scaffolding or workers added. One near mullion crosses the lens at the end to conceal the next cut. Five-second source clip.

## A04 / Setting the lite

> Tight three-quarter installation detail. Two anonymous gray-gloved hands grip black suction handles securely attached to one rigid clear glass lite. They make a small, controlled alignment movement toward the fixed graphite storefront channel; the pane remains supported and does not flex or float. Maintain correct reflections and the same sun direction as the reference. Camera makes a subtle forward push from outside the pane's path. End on the aligned vertical glass edge. Five-second source clip, no faces or full bodies.

## A05 / The last line

> Extreme macro of a sealant nozzle applying a continuous neat dark perimeter bead at a real glass-and-aluminum joint. Camera tracks at the nozzle's speed; reveal the finished bead behind the tip. The nozzle stays close to the substrate and the material adheres to the joint. Raking sunlight reveals texture without excessive wet shine. No extra fingers, no detached bead, no material melting through the frame. End on the completed dark vertical line. Five-second source clip.

## A06 / Turnover

> Begin on the same dark vertical in the completed storefront, then slowly dolly backward into a wide architectural view of the unchanged white facade. Clear glass lites, graphite double doors and accurate hardware are all installed. Soft warm interior lights balance the steel-blue reflections. Empty sidewalk; subtle natural tree movement at the edge only. End on a stable composition with clear negative space for typography, holding the last second without camera drift. No logo or lettering generated inside the clip. Five-second source clip.

**Post-production copy:** “We put the glass in.” Then the real CGC mark and `C-17 · LIC #1109661`. Put “Send plans for a bid” in the website UI, rather than baking a tiny button into the film.

# Film B / The Reflection Run

**24 seconds. Six shots.** The hook is a miniature world reflected in a glass edge. The camera follows the reflection until we realize we are inside the space. The finished building is revealed last. The entry and exit of each shot should share a reflection, edge or band of light to make the sequence feel continuous.

| Shot | Edit time | Camera and action | Transition | Sound idea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B01 | 00–04s | Macro travel along a polished glass edge; sunlight slides through the thickness. | Bright glint covers a cut to the facade. | Tiny crystalline note, soft low air movement. |
| B02 | 04–08s | Grazing lateral camera move along curtain-wall glass; reflected clouds shift in parallax. | A graphite mullion fills the screen. | Broad, restrained ambient chord. |
| B03 | 08–12s | Move past the mullion to an open door: reflection on the left, actual lobby on the right. | Cross the real open threshold. | Subtle outdoor-to-interior room-tone change. |
| B04 | 12–16s | Knee-height glide follows a geometric sun patch across terrazzo toward the office fronts. | A foreground glass edge creates another hidden cut. | Gentle foot-level room tone; no exaggerated whoosh. |
| B05 | 16–20s | Rise and arc around a frameless conference-room corner. Layers of reflection resolve into transparency. | Pull toward a window and cut on a reflected sky shape. | Brighter harmonics, light controlled movement. |
| B06 | 20–24s | Exterior pullback reveals the complete white office building at blue hour, warm interior lights visible. | Hold clean frame for real brand typography. | A warm final chord with a short natural decay. |

## Master prompt prefix

Append this to each shot prompt: “Photorealistic live-action architectural cinema, same white precast commercial office building, graphite aluminum details and steel-blue glass as the supplied reference, coherent reflected environment, realistic transparent glass thickness, no 3D render aesthetic, no duplicate architecture, no text, no logos, no people, no watermarks. Camera movement is physically smooth; the creative effect comes from reflection, focus and editorial match cuts, not warping solid objects.”

## B01 / A world in the edge

> Extreme macro along a polished clear-glass edge in morning light. A small reflected blue sky and white facade sit naturally within its thickness. The camera moves slowly lengthwise; a thin highlight travels over the bevel as the viewing angle changes. Shallow depth of field and fine material detail. Do not put a literal miniature building inside the pane. Finish on a controlled white glint occupying the central frame for a bright match cut. Five-second source clip.

## B02 / The moving mirror

> Start in the glint and settle into an oblique grazing view along the reference curtain-wall facade. Track left to right close to the surface. Real clouds and the white architecture across the street move in convincing reflection parallax, while the mullions remain rigid and consistent. One near mullion passes across the full lens at the end for a hidden cut. Avoid liquefying reflections, duplicate windows or a kaleidoscope. Five-second source clip.

## B03 / Reflection becomes a place

> Continue the same lateral travel past a dark entrance mullion. On the left we see the exterior reflected in a closed pane; on the right the real, already-open glass door reveals a bright white lobby. The camera arcs naturally through the existing doorway without crossing solid glass or changing the architecture. The transition from reflection to actual space happens through occlusion and parallax, not a portal effect. Five-second source clip.

## B04 / Follow the light

> Low, knee-height interior tracking shot over light terrazzo. A rectangular patch of sunlight falls through the reference entrance and leads toward clear glass office fronts. Glide forward with a slow rise, keeping verticals stable. The sun patch may drift subtly with camera perspective but must not behave like a glowing object on the floor. Finish as a nearby graphite glass edge crosses the lens. Five-second source clip.

## B05 / The invisible corner

> Start behind the foreground glass edge, then rise and arc 40 degrees around a frameless glass conference-room corner. Maintain one clean light-oak table and dark chairs behind the glass. At the beginning a reflected sky shape partly obscures the room; the change in angle resolves it into clear transparency. Glass edges remain sharp and physically fixed. No furniture morphing, extra doors, moving walls or floating panes. Five-second source clip.

## B06 / Reveal the building

> Exterior architectural pullback of the same reference white precast office building with its complete steel-blue curtain-wall glazing. Blue-hour sky, warm but restrained interior lighting, central glass double entrance and quiet clean sidewalk. Camera pulls back slowly and rises only slightly; no drone swoop or barrel roll. Preserve facade bay counts and the entrance position. Hold the final composition with room for the headline to be added in editing. Five-second source clip.

**Post-production copy:** “We put the glass in.” The real CGC logo appears after the reveal; small supporting text may read “Commercial glazing / Southern California.” Do not add made-up client signs to the building.

## Shorter edits

- **12-second website loop:** B01 → B03 → B05 → B06, around three seconds each. Fade the final reflection into the opening glint. Keep controls outside the video.
- **15-second social teaser:** A01 → A02 → A04 → A05 → A06, roughly three seconds each. Use the pencil-to-metal match as the first-second hook.
- **6-second bumper:** B01 macro glint → B06 facade, with the real logo applied in post. No tiny text or rapid flicker.

## Creative extensions

**The room that wasn't there:** Start on an ordinary open conference table. A slow orbit reveals existing glass only when reflections catch its edges. End looking through the newly defined room at the same table. Reveal through camera angle, not walls magically materializing.

**One opening, one day:** Repeat the exact locked camera at dawn, noon and blue hour, using mullions as edit wipes between work stages. The camera never changes; the light and opening do. Generate each stage from the same reference instead of asking one clip to remodel the building continuously.

**The light signature:** A narrow rectangular sunlight shape recurs on paper, metal, the floor and finally the whole entrance. This motif can link the homepage film, social clips and generated stills without relying on stock construction montages.
