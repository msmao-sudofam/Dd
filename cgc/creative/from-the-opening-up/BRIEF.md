# FROM THE OPENING UP

## CGC / ground-up storefront installation film

An empty opening becomes a finished glass storefront through work you can see: set the sill, align the frame, seat the glass, finish the joint, fit the door. The final reveal is earned by those physical actions.

Creative line: Built from the opening up.

This is a new alternate concept for Commercial Glazing Contractors Inc., not a rendered video or a record of an actual CGC project. It covers installation of a storefront into an existing commercial building shell. It does not depict CGC constructing the building or pouring its foundations. The approximate opening dimensions below are fictional scene-design dimensions, not claims about a CGC job.

## Film structure

Eight shots. Twenty-four seconds. Silent website master at 24fps, 576 frames. Also export a sound-design version for presentation if wanted. White stucco, graphite aluminum, clear glass and restrained steel-blue reflections. Bright neutral daylight throughout. The excitement comes from material, weight, alignment and the change in the opening, not flash transitions.

The storyboard reads left to right, top to bottom. It is a visual direction sheet, not a set of production-ready keyframes. Panel 02 includes an upright jamb although the shot should isolate the sill before upright framing; correct this in its clean start frame. Panel 05 is illustrative of handling but needs a clearly visible gap above the sill before seating. Panel 07 needs a visible gap between the supported door leaf and its jamb. Do not animate the storyboard collage, panel numbers or these ambiguous poses.

## Lock the storefront before generating

Use the overall architecture of panel 08 as the visual direction. Make a clean straight-on final elevation first. Opening approximately 24 feet wide by 10 feet high; white stucco piers at both sides; flat graphite fascia above; pale concrete sidewalk; same unfinished retail interior behind it. Fix one horizontal transom, two fixed-glass bays at left and one narrower single-door bay at right. Choose and record exact normalized mullion positions in the final elevation, then hold them for every stage. No second door, additional window or changing transom height.

Create three aligned wide continuity plates with the same lens, tripod position and architecture: empty opening, complete frame with empty pockets, completed glazing with door closed. Derive stages from the same reference. Do not independently invent eight buildings from text. The generated board has small geometry differences and is not the geometry authority.

Use a flat-on 35mm-equivalent wide view as the continuity anchor; close-ups are separately composed from that geometry. Daylight comes from upper left, the sidewalk and roof remain fixed, and exterior reflections must match the surrounding scene. No logos, readable signs, client branding or invented address. No change to sunset, trees or occupied showroom between stages.

Workers: two adult installers in plain charcoal/gray workwear, hard hats, eye protection and gloves. Keep wardrobe and number consistent. Avoid face close-ups. Hands hold tools or handles with stable contact. Glass lites remain rigid rectangles with plausible thickness and edges. Support and handling must look credible; do not use precarious overhead loads or workers under suspended glass. This is cinematic direction, not a field installation procedure. Have a glazing professional review the depicted details before publishing them as representative work.

## Global acceptance gates

- A locked-camera shot has no intentional pan, truck, dolly or zoom. Only the prescribed worker/object action moves. Reject camera movement even if the clip looks attractive.
- I01 requires a real rightward truck. I08 requires a real pullback. Near and far geometry must move differently through parallax. Digital scale, an animated crop or a held image fails.
- Panels do not grow, bend, liquefy, merge into hands, change tint or pass through metal. Aluminum members do not appear out of nowhere.
- Each installation action starts visibly incomplete, progresses physically and ends visibly closer to the intended installed state. A stationary installer pose with flickering hands fails.
- Cuts may omit elapsed work between operations. Individual clips must not transform the whole facade to jump between construction stages.
- Keep the same opening, mullion layout, door side, light direction and crew. Reject a handsome shot of a different storefront.
- All eight shots must pass their individual action and geometry checks. A mean quality score cannot hide a failed mandatory action.

For every attempt extract first, 25%, 50%, 75% and last frames plus a contact sheet. Watch the complete clip at normal speed and half speed. Check rigid edges, hand contact, camera direction, glass/frame clearance, continuity and end state. Frame samples alone cannot prove correct movement. If available, use point tracks or optical flow as supporting evidence; visual inspection of the clip remains required. Record pass/fail reasons, not just adjectives such as cinematic.
