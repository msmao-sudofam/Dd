# Pattern checklists

These are general conversion and design conventions, used to check a build. They
are not taken from the galleries. Live references from the galleries come first.
Use these lists to review a draft, or alone only when no gallery could be
reached.

## Landing page (land-book)
- Hero → social proof → problem/benefits → how it works → features → proof → pricing/offer → FAQ → final CTA → footer.
- One primary CTA, repeated with the same wording, never more than a scroll away.
- Every section answers one question; its headline can be read on its own.
- Use one type family (two at most), a clear scale, and generous section spacing.

## Hero (supahero)
- Headline names the outcome for the reader, ideally in ≤ 10 words. A subhead says how / for whom.
- One primary CTA plus at most one secondary (demo, "see how it works").
- Proof sits close to the CTA: logos, rating, user count.
- The visual shows the product or the result, not decoration.
- It works at 375px wide: headline wraps cleanly and the CTA is visible without scrolling.

## Pricing (pricingpages)
- 3 tiers is the default. Highlight the recommended tier with a badge and contrast.
- Monthly/annual toggle with the savings shown ("2 months free").
- Lead each tier with who it's for. Features are cumulative ("Everything in Pro, plus…").
- Comparison table below the cards; FAQ and a risk reducer (trial, guarantee, cancel anytime) below that.
- Enterprise / "Talk to sales" sits apart from the self-serve tiers.

## Social proof (socialproofexamples)
- Specific beats generic: name, role, company, photo, and a concrete result.
- Pick a format for the job: logo wall (credibility), metric strip (scale), testimonial cards (relatability), case-study snippets (depth), tweet/review wall (volume), ratings badges (trust).
- Put proof right after a claim or next to a CTA, not only in one block.

## Email (reallygoodemails)
- Subject and preheader work as a pair. Preview text is not wasted.
- One goal and one primary CTA (button, not a text link) above the fold.
- Single column, about 600px, live text instead of text baked into images, and alt text on images.
- Scannable: short blocks, clear headers, footer with unsubscribe and address.

## Deck / report (deck.gallery)
- One idea per slide, written as a headline sentence ("Revenue doubled in Q3"), not a topic label.
- A consistent grid, a limited palette, and large type for the key figure.
- Cover → agenda → narrative → evidence → ask/summary.

## Social post (posts.design)
- The hook must be readable in under a second at thumbnail size.
- Brand stays consistent across the feed: palette, type, and the logo placed the same way.
- Carousel: slide 1 hooks, slides 2–n deliver, the last slide is a CTA.
- Size for the platform (1080×1350 IG feed, 1080×1920 stories/reels, 1200×627 LinkedIn).

## Paid ad (adfolio)
- One message per ad, and the hook lands in the first second or line.
- A visual pattern-interrupt: bold colour, a face, an unexpected object, a big number.
- The offer and CTA are explicit. Copy matches the landing page it links to.
- Little text on the image; check the platform's safe zones.

## Navbar (navbar.gallery)
- Logo left, 4–7 top-level items, primary CTA on the right with a contrasting style.
- Mega-menus group items under headings, use icons and one-line descriptions, and can include a featured card.
- Sticky, with a reduced height on scroll. Mobile gets a full-screen or drawer menu with tap targets ≥ 44px.

## OG image (ogimage.gallery)
- 1200×630, with the important content inside a centre-safe area (crops vary).
- Large title (readable at about 300px wide), the brand mark, and high contrast.
- One per page/post (templated) rather than a single site-wide image.
- Set `og:image`, `og:title`, `og:description` and `twitter:card=summary_large_image`.
